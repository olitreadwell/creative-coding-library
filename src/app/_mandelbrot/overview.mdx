## What this is

The Mandelbrot set is one of the most famous pictures in math. It looks like a dark, bumpy beetle with endless curls around the edge. Zoom into the edge and you find more curls, forever. The picture never runs out of detail.

The cool part: the whole thing comes from one tiny rule, repeated many times.

## The big idea in plain words

Every pixel on the screen stands for a point. For each point we play a small game:

- Start at zero.
- Apply the same step again and again.
- Watch what happens to the number.

For some points the number stays small and calm. Those points are **inside** the set, and we paint them black. For other points the number blows up fast and runs off to infinity. Those are **outside** the set. We give them a color based on **how fast** they ran away.

That speed is the whole trick. Points that escape quickly get one color. Points that hang on longer get another. Lined up across the screen, those colors draw the glowing edge you see.

## Why it matters

This is a first taste of a few deep ideas, in a friendly form:

- **Feedback loops.** Feeding a result back into the same rule can create shapes far more complex than the rule itself.
- **Complex numbers.** The points live on a 2D number plane, not a 1D line. That second dimension is what makes the curls possible.
- **Fractals.** Detail that repeats at every zoom level. The coastline of the set is infinitely long.
