## What this is

Webcam Mirror takes the live feed from your camera and redraws it as a grid of colored dots. Each dot sits at the center of a small square of pixels. The bigger and brighter the dot, the brighter that part of the image was. Dark areas disappear; bright areas glow.

You stay in control. The camera does not start until you click "Enable camera." You can close the tab at any time to stop the feed.

## The big idea in plain words

A video frame is just a big grid of colored pixels. Each pixel has a red value, a green value, and a blue value. From those three numbers we can calculate a single number called **luminance**, which tells us how bright that pixel is on a scale from 0 (black) to 1 (white).

We group the pixels into cells, one per dot. We sample the color at that cell, calculate the luminance, and then draw a circle. A luminance of 0 gives a dot with radius 0, invisible. A luminance of 1 gives the largest circle that fits inside the cell.

The color of each dot comes from a **colorblind-safe sequential ramp** that runs from deep blue (dark areas) to bright yellow (highlights). This ramp stays readable for people with protanopia or deuteranopia because it avoids the red-green axis entirely.

The image is **mirrored horizontally** so it matches what you expect from a mirror, not a camera facing you.

## Controls

- **Dot size** changes how many columns and rows of dots fit on the canvas. Larger dots give a coarser, more abstract look. Smaller dots reveal more detail.
- **Contrast** multiplies each luminance value before it becomes a dot radius. Higher values push mid-tones toward full brightness and make the overall image punchier.

## Why it matters

This sketch introduces two important web concepts. First, `getUserMedia` is the browser API that requests a live video stream from the camera. It always asks the user for permission, and the browser enforces that permission natively. Second, drawing a video frame onto a canvas and reading pixels back with `getImageData` is how many real-time image effects work in the browser, from face filters to motion detection.
