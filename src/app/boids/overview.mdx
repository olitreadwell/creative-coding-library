# Boids

A simulation where triangles flock together like birds, using only three local rules.

Learners see how complex group behavior can grow from simple individual rules, with no central controller.

## What you see

- A group of small triangles moving across the canvas, forming loose flocks.
- The flock splits and rejoins as triangles navigate around each other.
- Triangles wrap off one edge and reappear on the opposite side.
- Each triangle is colored by its heading and speed.

## What it teaches

- **Emergence:** the flock appears without any code that says "flock together." Three small rules produce it.
- **Separation, alignment, cohesion:** the three steering forces every boid applies each frame.
- **Vectors:** each boid stores a velocity as two numbers, `vx` and `vy`, and adds small nudges to them.
- **Seeded randomness:** the same seed always produces the same starting flock.

## Try this

- Click "New flock" to get a different starting arrangement and watch how the personality of the flock changes.
- Watch for the moment when two sub-flocks merge. Notice that no single boid decided to join.
- Cover most of the screen mentally and watch one boid: it responds only to what is nearby, not the whole flock.
