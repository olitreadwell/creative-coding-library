# Tutorial: Boids

By the end of this tutorial you will be able to explain what each of the three boid rules does and predict what happens when one is removed.

## Predict

Each boid follows three rules: move away from neighbors that are too close, steer toward the average direction of nearby neighbors, and steer toward the average position of nearby neighbors. The flock has no leader.

If you removed the separation rule but kept the other two, what would happen to the flock over time? Think about it before reading on.

## Run

1. Press Play and watch for about ten seconds. Notice that the triangles form groups without being told to.
2. Click "New flock" to start with a different seed. Same rules, different starting positions, different result.
3. Watch one triangle near the edge of a flock. Watch it turn as the group turns. That turn came from alignment.
4. Watch two groups collide. Some triangles separate from the group for a moment before being drawn back. That is separation and cohesion working at the same time.

## Look closer

- The edges of the canvas are toroidal, meaning a boid that exits the right side enters from the left. Boids near the boundary treat boids on the opposite edge as neighbors.
- Color comes from heading and speed. A boid traveling fast in one direction looks different from one hovering slowly.
- Each boid scans all other boids every frame, but only responds to those inside its perception radius. Boids outside that radius are invisible to it.
- The three force weights (separation, alignment, cohesion) use small multipliers. If one weight were too large it would overpower the others and break the flock.

## Break it

- Try disabling cohesion in the source code: set the cohesion weight to 0. The flock should scatter and never regroup.
- Try setting the perception radius very small: boids become nearly blind and the flock dissolves into independent wanderers.
- Try setting separation weight to 0: boids should collapse into a dense clump.
- Try `New flock` ten times in a row. Count how many distinct sub-flocks you see in each run.

## Recap

Three local rules, applied independently by each boid every frame, produce a flock with no central controller. That is emergence: the whole is more organized than any single rule.
