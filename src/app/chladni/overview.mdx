# Chladni Figures

A simulation of the glowing lines that appear on a vibrating plate, computed from one trigonometry formula.

Learners see how real physics patterns can come from a single equation, with no special effects or image files.

## What you see

- Bright curved lines on a dark background, slowly breathing and shifting.
- The pattern reorganizes when you move the mode sliders, snapping to a new shape.
- Lines are always symmetric, and the outer edge of the plate always forms a boundary.
- On low "reduce motion" settings, the figure holds still.

## What it teaches

- **Nodes and standing waves:** the bright lines mark places on the plate that never move when it vibrates.
- **Superposition:** two sine-wave patterns are added together. The result is their sum.
- **Trigonometry in practice:** the entire pattern comes from products of sine functions evaluated on a grid.
- **Parameters:** changing `a` or `b` by one integer reorganizes the whole figure.

## Try this

- Set both mode sliders to the same number and look for diagonal symmetry.
- Drag one slider slowly from 1 to 8 and watch the number of segments multiply.
- Set `a = 4, b = 7` for a dense lattice, or `a = 1, b = 2` for the simplest cross shape.
