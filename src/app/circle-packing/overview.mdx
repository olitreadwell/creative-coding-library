# Circle Packing

Circles grow one at a time until they touch a neighbor or the edge, filling a canvas without overlapping.

Learners see how a simple greedy rule, applied repeatedly, produces a structured result that looks designed.

## What you see

- Circles appear one by one and grow outward until they stop.
- Early circles are large because they have more open space. Later circles are small because they squeeze into gaps.
- Each circle is colored by size: small circles are cool, large circles are warm.
- Pressing "Shuffle" clears the canvas and starts again with a different seed.

## What it teaches

- **Greedy algorithms:** each circle grows to the largest safe size without looking ahead.
- **Seeded randomness:** the same seed always produces the same packing, every time.
- **Constraint satisfaction:** every circle obeys one rule (no overlap) and the result fills the space efficiently.
- **Smoothstep:** circles appear with a soft easing animation rather than snapping into place.

## Try this

- Click "Shuffle" several times and compare how different seeds produce different size distributions.
- Drag the "density" slider high to try more candidate positions and fill the gaps more completely.
- Set "min size" to a small value and zoom into the corners. Many tiny circles fill the cracks.
