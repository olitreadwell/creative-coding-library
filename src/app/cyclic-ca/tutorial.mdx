# Tutorial: Cyclic Automaton

By the end of this tutorial you will understand why spirals appear and how changing the threshold changes their shape.

## Predict

Each cell counts how many of its eight neighbors hold the state that "eats" it. If that count reaches the threshold, the cell changes to that state. Otherwise it stays.

With a threshold of 3, a cell needs at least three neighbors in the eating state before it changes. With a threshold of 1, it changes if even one neighbor holds the eating state.

Which threshold do you think creates faster, sharper spiral fronts: 1 or 3? Think about how easily one state can spread into another.

## Run

1. Open the sketch with 5 states and threshold 3. Let it run for ten seconds. Note the shape of the spiral arms.
2. Click "Randomize" to reset. Change threshold to 1. Let it run for ten seconds. Compare the spiral arms.
3. Increase states to 7. Let it run. Notice how the arms become more numerous and thinner.
4. Click "Randomize" several times with states at 5 and threshold at 2. Notice that spirals always appear, even though the starting grid is different each time.

## Look closer

- The grid wraps at all four edges (toroidal). A cell at the right edge counts cells at the left edge as neighbors. Spirals can cross boundaries without distortion.
- The simulation time-steps at a fixed interval, not at the frame rate. This keeps the speed consistent even on a fast monitor.
- Colors come from the Okabe-Ito colorblind-safe palette. Each state maps to a distinct hue that remains readable for the most common forms of color vision difference.
- Spirals form because a curved advancing front is stable. A straight front is not. Small random irregularities in the starting grid are enough to start the curving.

## Break it

- Try threshold 4 with 5 states. The eating slows down and the spirals form more slowly and loosely.
- Try 3 states and threshold 1. There are very few spiral arms because there are fewer states competing.
- Let the simulation run for a long time without resetting. Do the spirals ever stop changing, or do they rotate indefinitely?
- Try 8 states and threshold 2. Compare the density of spiral arms to 5 states.

## Recap

A cyclic eating rule, applied independently by each cell every tick, produces rotating spirals with no central design. The spirals appear because curved fronts are stable and straight fronts are not.
