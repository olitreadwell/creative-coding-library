## What this is

A chain of rotating circles, each spinning at a different speed, whose combined tip traces out a shape. The shape shown here is a square wave, a signal that jumps sharply between two values. The circles alone are smooth and round. Together they draw something with corners. That gap between the simple pieces and the complex result is the whole point.

## The big idea in plain words

Imagine attaching a stick to a spinning wheel. The end of the stick draws a circle. Now attach another stick to the end of that first stick, spinning at a different speed and size. The end of the second stick draws a more complicated loop. Keep adding sticks and the shape gets more intricate.

That is an epicycle. Do it with enough sticks and the right speeds, and you can trace any closed shape you like.

The trick for choosing the speeds and sizes is the Fourier series. It tells you exactly which circles to use for any shape you want to draw:

- **Frequency** is how fast a circle spins. The fundamental circle completes one rotation per full cycle. The next adds three rotations. Then five, then seven. Only odd-numbered frequencies appear in a square wave.
- **Amplitude** is the radius of each circle. Bigger radius means that circle contributes more to the shape. The fundamental circle has the biggest radius. Each higher harmonic is smaller by the same factor it is faster.
- **Phase** sets where on the circle each arm starts at time zero.

## Why it matters

This is one of the most useful ideas in all of math and engineering:

- **Signal processing.** Sound, radio, images: all analyzed and reconstructed by breaking them into frequency components, exactly as shown here.
- **Convergence.** Adding more circles brings the approximation closer to the target shape. You can see the sharp corners of the square wave becoming crisper as the higher-frequency, smaller circles kick in.
- **Any shape.** The result is not just for square waves. Given enough terms you can draw a circle, a star, a letter, or a face. The mathematics is the same.
