# Fourier Epicycles

A chain of rotating circles whose combined tip traces a square wave, showing how smooth rotation produces sharp corners.

Learners see the Fourier series (a way to break any repeating shape into rotating circles) made visible in real time.

## What you see

- A chain of circles, each spinning inside the previous one, with the tip of the last arm drawing a path.
- The traced path slowly fills in to form a square wave shape.
- Smaller, faster circles add detail at the corners.
- After one full cycle, the path closes on itself.

## What it teaches

- **Fourier series:** any repeating shape can be described as a sum of rotating circles of different sizes and speeds.
- **Frequency and amplitude:** the speed of each circle (frequency) and its size (amplitude) together determine its contribution.
- **Parametric curves:** both x and y are computed from a shared variable `t`, not from a single equation `y = f(x)`.
- **Convergence:** adding more circles brings the approximation closer to the target shape.

## Try this

- Watch the first few seconds. The large, slow circle alone draws a rough circle. As more circles join in, the shape approaches a square.
- Count the sharp bumps in the traced path. Each bump corresponds to one additional circle completing a rotation.
- Press "Reset" and watch the path rebuild from the tip of the chain outward.
