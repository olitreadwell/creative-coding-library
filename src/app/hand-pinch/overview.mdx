## What this is

Control a sketch with your hands, no mouse. Turn on the camera, then pinch your
thumb and finger together to grab a soft blob. Move your pinch to drag it. Pinch
with both hands and pull apart to stretch the blob between them. Your camera feed
is shown faintly behind, with dots marking the points the tracker found on each
hand.

Nothing leaves your browser. The camera and the hand tracker only start after you
press the button.

## The big idea in plain words

A hand-tracking model looks at each camera frame and returns 21 points per hand:
the wrist, knuckles, and fingertips, each as an x/y position. With those points
you can read gestures. The simplest is the pinch: measure the distance between
the thumb tip and the index fingertip. When that distance is small, the fingers
are touching, so the user is pinching. The midpoint of the two tips is the
"grab point".

From there the interaction is ordinary 2D maths: one pinch sets the blob's
position; two pinches set the two opposite edges, so the blob stretches to fit
between your hands. A little easing each frame makes the motion feel soft instead
of jumpy.

## Why it matters

Camera-based input opens interactions that a mouse cannot: reach out and grab,
pull two things apart, wave. The hand model does the hard computer-vision work;
your job is to turn its points into meaning. The pinch is the "click" of hand
tracking, and once you can detect it you can build drawing tools, instruments,
and games that respond to real gestures.
