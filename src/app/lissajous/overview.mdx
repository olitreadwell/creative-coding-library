# Lissajous

A glowing curve traced by a point moving in two sine waves at once, slowly morphing as the phase between them shifts.

Learners see one of the clearest examples of a parametric curve: both x and y computed from a single shared variable, producing shapes that look almost hand-drawn.

## What you see

- A glowing loop on a dark background, continuously shifting in shape.
- The curve rotates and stretches as time passes, never repeating exactly.
- The outer loops fade toward the center, giving a harmonograph-like decay.
- Four ratio buttons (3:2, 5:4, 3:4, 5:6) each produce a different family of shapes.

## What it teaches

- **Parametric curve:** both x and y are computed from a shared variable `t`, not from a single equation `y = f(x)`.
- **Frequency ratio:** the ratio of the two sine wave frequencies sets the basic shape. 3:2 makes a different shape from 5:4.
- **Phase:** a time offset between the two sine waves. Advancing it each frame makes the shape continuously morph.
- **Additive blending:** overlapping line segments add their colors together, creating bright glowing intersections.

## Try this

- Switch between the four ratio buttons and watch the shape change. Notice which ratios produce simple loops and which produce complex figures.
- Watch one shape for a full minute. Does it ever return to exactly where it started?
- Switch from 3:2 to 5:4 while the animation is running. The shape morphs without jumping because the phase continues from where it left off.
