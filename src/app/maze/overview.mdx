## What this is

A maze is a network of passages with exactly one route between any two points. No loops. No dead ends that cut the maze in two. Every cell connects to every other cell through some chain of passages. That property is called a **perfect maze**, and it is what you are watching being built.

The maze on screen is generated by a computer algorithm that carves passages one at a time, starting from a corner and working its way through the entire grid before it is done. You can watch it happen live.

## The big idea in plain words

Think of the grid as a map of rooms, all with four locked doors (north, east, south, west). The algorithm picks a room, randomly knocks down one of its locked doors to reach a new room, and moves there. If it gets stuck (all neighbors already visited), it backs up and tries again. It keeps going until every room has been reached.

The result is a tree, not a tangle. There is exactly one path from any room to any other. That is what makes it a perfect maze.

## Why it matters

This is a small, complete example of three ideas that show up all over computing:

- **Graphs.** The grid of rooms is a graph: nodes connected by edges. Maze generation is really about building a spanning tree of a grid graph.
- **Depth-first search.** The algorithm always goes as deep as possible before backtracking. That search pattern is one of the most useful tools in computer science.
- **Backtracking.** When a path hits a dead end, you undo your last choice and try another. The same idea powers puzzle solvers, compilers, and AI planning.

The maze is the algorithm made visible. Watch the frontier (the highlighted cell) push into unexplored space, then snap back along a thread when it gets cornered. That snap is backtracking.
