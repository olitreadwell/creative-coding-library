## What this is

Mic Spectrum listens to your microphone and draws a live bar chart of the sound
it hears. Bass sits on the left, treble on the right, and each bar grows taller
when that part of the sound gets louder. Click "Enable microphone", then speak,
play music, or snap near the mic.

Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. The audio stays in your browser and the
microphone only turns on after you press the button.

## The big idea in plain words

Sound is never just one note at one volume. A voice or a chord is many
frequencies happening at once. The Web Audio API splits that mix into bins with
an FFT (a Fast Fourier Transform), and each bin says how much energy sits in a
narrow band of pitches.

Read those bins every frame and paint one bar per bin, and you can watch a sound
come apart into its pieces in real time.

## Why it matters

Four small steps unlock a whole category of work: ask for microphone access,
build a tiny audio graph, read the FFT each frame, and draw it. The same steps
power music visualizers, equalizers, voice-activated effects, and tuning tools.

The How it works tab walks through the code.
