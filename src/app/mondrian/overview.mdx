# Mondrian

An endless tunnel of Mondrian-style grid panels flying toward you, with perspective scaling and depth-of-field blur.

Learners see how three independent ideas (recursive subdivision, perspective projection, and depth of field) combine to produce a convincing 3D effect on a flat canvas.

## What you see

- A continuous stream of colorful grid panels rushing toward the camera.
- Panels near the focus depth look sharp and fully opaque. Distant panels look faint.
- Each panel is a unique Mondrian-style layout: primary-colored rectangles divided by thick black lines.
- Clicking the canvas scrambles all layers with new random layouts.

## What it teaches

- **Perspective projection:** the formula `scale = focal / (focal + z)` makes far panels small and near panels large.
- **Recursive subdivision:** the `subdivide` function splits a rectangle randomly, then calls itself on each half, producing a tree of rectangles.
- **Depth of field:** alpha (transparency) is mapped to distance from the focus depth, simulating a camera's focus window.
- **Recycling:** panels that pass the camera are assigned a new seed and placed at the far end of the tunnel, creating an endless loop from a finite pool.

## Try this

- Drag "focus" from 0 to 2000 and watch the sharpness window sweep through the tunnel.
- Set "density" to 2 for large, bold blocks per panel.
- Set "speed" to maximum and watch the tunnel blur into a pattern.
