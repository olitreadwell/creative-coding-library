## What this is

A noise field is a canvas full of particles. Thousands of tiny dots are placed at random positions. Each frame, every dot moves one small step. The direction of that step comes from a "flow field" built from Perlin noise. The result looks like smoke, flowing water, or wind patterns on a weather map.

The sketch runs in your browser using Canvas 2D. No library handles the physics. The code does all the work.

## The big idea in plain words

There are two main ingredients.

**Perlin noise** is a way to make smooth, random-looking numbers. Regular randomness jumps all over the place. Perlin noise changes slowly and gradually, so nearby positions get similar values. Think of it like a gentle landscape of hills and valleys instead of a spike chart.

**A flow field** turns those noise values into directions. For every point on the canvas, we sample the noise at that position and convert the result to an angle. That angle tells a particle, "go this way." Because the noise is smooth, nearby particles get similar angles, so they tend to flow together rather than scatter.

Each particle draws a thin line from where it was to where it is now. The lines are painted with additive blending: where many lines overlap, the colors add up and glow brighter. Over time, the canvas fills with a luminous pattern of streams.

## Why it matters

This sketch shows three ideas that come up constantly in creative coding and simulation:

- **Smooth randomness.** Perlin noise is the workhorse of procedural generation. Terrain, clouds, organic shapes, and animations all use it.
- **Vector fields.** Assigning a direction to every point in space is a technique used in physics simulations, fluid dynamics, and data visualization.
- **Trails and blending.** Additive blending is a simple trick that makes overlapping thin lines look like glowing light. It shows up in particle systems, data art, and shader programming.

You do not need to understand the math to watch it run. The Tutorial tab walks through the code, one step at a time.
