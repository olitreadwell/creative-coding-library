# Phyllotaxis

The spiral packing pattern found in sunflowers, pinecones, and pineapples, drawn from two lines of math and one special angle.

Learners see why the golden angle (approximately 137.5 degrees) produces even packing, and what happens when you move slightly away from it.

## What you see

- A bloom of colored dots packed in interlocking spirals.
- Scrolling up adds more seeds; scrolling down removes them.
- The angle slider shifts the pattern: near 137.5 degrees you see even spirals, at other angles you see spokes and gaps.
- The bloom slowly rotates.

## What it teaches

- **Golden angle:** rotating each new seed by approximately 137.5 degrees places it in the largest remaining gap, producing even packing.
- **Square root spacing:** seed `i` sits at distance `sqrt(i)` from the center. This keeps the area between seeds roughly equal at all distances.
- **Polar to Cartesian:** each seed's angle and distance are converted to x and y using `cos` and `sin`.
- **Scroll interactivity:** a wheel event listener calls `preventDefault()` to control seed count without scrolling the page.

## Try this

- Scroll to grow the bloom from a handful of seeds to thousands.
- Set the angle exactly to 137.5 degrees, then nudge it by 0.1 degree and watch the spirals collapse into spokes.
- Try 90 degrees and compare the regular grid that appears to the uneven spiral at 137.5 degrees.
