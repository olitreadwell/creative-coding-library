## What this is

The spiral pattern you see on a sunflower head, a pinecone, or a pineapple. Each
dot is a seed, and every new seed is placed a fixed turn around the center from
the last one. Pick the right turn and the seeds pack into the interlocking
spirals nature uses. Scroll up or down on the canvas to add or remove seeds and
watch the bloom grow or shrink.

## The big idea in plain words

Two rules place every seed:

1. **Turn by a fixed angle.** Seed number i sits at angle `i × angle` around the
   center.
2. **Step outward.** Its distance from the center is `sqrt(i)`, which keeps the
   seeds evenly dense instead of bunching up.

The magic is in the angle. Use a whole-number fraction of a circle, like 90 or
120 degrees, and the seeds line up into a few obvious arms with big gaps between
them. Use the **golden angle**, about 137.5 degrees, and no two seeds ever line
up, so they fill the space evenly and the eye picks out spirals running both
ways. Slide the angle control a hair off 137.5 and the whole pattern reorganizes,
which shows how precise nature's choice is.

## Why it matters

Phyllotaxis is one of the clearest places where a simple rule meets a deep
number. The golden angle comes from the golden ratio, and plants use it because
even seed packing means more seeds in less space. For creative coding it is a
tiny recipe, two lines of trigonometry, that yields a rich, organic image you can
play with by ear.
