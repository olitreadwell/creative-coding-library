# Physics Drops

Colorful circles and polygons fall, bounce, and pile up under simulated gravity using the matter.js physics engine.

Learners see how a physics engine separates the job of computing physics from the job of drawing, and what the three core concepts (engine, world, bodies) mean in practice.

## What you see

- Shapes dropping from the top of the canvas one at a time.
- Shapes bouncing off each other and off the invisible walls and floor.
- The pile growing until the container is roughly 60% full, then stopping.
- Sliders controlling gravity strength and bounciness.

## What it teaches

- **Physics engine:** software that simulates gravity, collision, and stacking. You describe objects; the engine computes the results.
- **Bodies:** objects in the simulation. Each has a shape, mass, position, and physical properties.
- **Static bodies:** walls and floors marked as immovable. Other bodies collide with them but they never move.
- **Data and rendering separation:** the engine works with numbers. The renderer reads those numbers and draws. Keeping them separate lets you swap one without touching the other.

## Try this

- Drag "size" up and press "Reset." Bigger shapes fill the container with fewer items.
- Lower "bounciness" and the shapes settle with almost no jitter. Raise it and the pile bounces chaotically.
- Click "Reset" several times. A new seed picks new colors and positions each time.
