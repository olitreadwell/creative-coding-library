# Tutorial: Pointer Flow

By the end of this tutorial you will understand how the shockwave produces a timed impulse per balloon and how the squash spring restores the balloon shape.

## Predict

The shockwave ring expands outward at a fixed speed. Its impulse on a balloon is computed using a Gaussian (bell-curve) shape centered on the ring edge. The impulse is near zero when the ring is far from the balloon center.

If the ring expands at 400 pixels per second and a balloon is 300 pixels from the click point, roughly how many seconds pass before the ring reaches that balloon?

## Run

1. Click in an empty area. Watch the ring expand outward. Count approximately how many balloons it passes.
2. Click very close to one balloon. The squash should be immediate and strong.
3. Click far from all balloons. The ring passes them with a lighter impulse because the ring is fading with age.
4. Switch to repel mode and move the cursor slowly toward a group of balloons. Watch them spread apart as you approach.

## Look closer

- Velocity is multiplied by 0.82 each frame, regardless of forces. This is friction. Without it, balloons would accelerate indefinitely.
- The squash value is a scalar on the balloon's shape. When the ring hits, it kicks the squash downward. The spring equation `squash += (1 - squash) * restoreRate` gradually brings it back to 1 (round).
- Each balloon has a per-balloon wobble phase. The wobble adds a small sine offset to the radius of each point in the balloon shape, so each blob looks independently alive.
- A shockwave ring expires after 1.4 seconds. Rings that age past that point are removed from the array each frame.

## Break it

- Click rapidly in the same spot five times. Watch the balloons receive multiple impulses in quick succession.
- Set strength to maximum. Balloons should fly fast and the squash should be dramatic.
- Set the balloon count to 1 and click around it repeatedly. Study the squash-and-spring behavior in isolation.
- Hold the button down and drag slowly. Notice how overlapping rings from the drag trail combine.

## Recap

Each click spawns a ring. The ring expands. Each frame, each balloon checks how close the ring edge is and takes a proportional impulse. The squash spring restores the shape. Three forces (cursor, ring, soft separation) plus friction produce the full behavior.
