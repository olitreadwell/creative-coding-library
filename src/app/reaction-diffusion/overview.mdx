# Reaction Diffusion

Two virtual chemicals spread and react on a grid, growing spots, stripes, and maze-like patterns from a nearly blank starting state.

Learners see the Gray-Scott model, a simulation proposed in 1984 that produces patterns found on animal coats and in chemical laboratory reactions.

## What you see

- A grid that starts mostly uniform, with a few seeded patches.
- Over several seconds, patterns grow outward from the seeds.
- The patterns can look like spots, stripes, mazes, or branching webs, depending on the parameters.
- Pressing "Reset" generates a new set of seeds and lets the patterns grow from scratch.

## What it teaches

- **Reaction diffusion:** two chemicals (called A and B) spread (diffuse) and convert into each other (react) at each step.
- **Gray-Scott model:** a specific set of equations that uses a feed rate (how fast fresh A enters) and a kill rate (how fast B is removed).
- **Laplacian:** the mathematical operation that models diffusion by averaging a cell's value with its neighbors.
- **Emergence:** complex living-looking patterns grow from three ideas: A spreads faster than B, B is created where A and B meet, and B is removed at a fixed rate.

## Try this

- Click "Reset" several times and watch different seed arrangements produce different final patterns.
- Change the feed rate by a small amount and compare the pattern character: more dots, fewer dots, or stripes.
- Set steps-per-frame higher to watch the pattern form faster.
