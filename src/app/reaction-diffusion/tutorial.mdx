# Tutorial: Reaction Diffusion

By the end of this tutorial you will understand what the feed and kill rates control and why A diffuses faster than B.

## Predict

Chemical A starts everywhere at 1.0 (maximum). Chemical B starts at 0 everywhere, except in a few seeded patches. The reaction converts A into B wherever A and B are both present.

If A diffuses much faster than B, which chemical do you expect to spread further from a seeded patch: A or B? Think about which one has more mobility.

## Run

1. Open the sketch and watch for ten seconds. Note what shape the first patterns take.
2. Press "Reset" and watch again. The shapes start from different seed positions but follow the same rules.
3. Wait two minutes without resetting. The pattern continues to evolve and fill the canvas.
4. Open the source file `grayscott.ts` and change `feed` from 0.055 to 0.04. Reset and compare.

## Look closer

- The simulation runs on a 200 by 150 grid, not at full screen resolution. This small grid is scaled up with `drawImage` to fill the screen. The CSS property `imageRendering: pixelated` keeps cell edges crisp rather than blurred.
- Each update step clamps both A and B to the range [0, 1]. This prevents floating-point drift from accumulating across thousands of frames.
- The Laplacian for a cell is computed as a weighted sum: the center cell subtracts its own value, and the eight neighbors add a weighted share. Cardinal neighbors (up, down, left, right) count twice as much as diagonals.
- Multiple steps run per frame. Running the simulation several times before drawing gives the impression of faster development without changing the frame rate.

## Break it

- Change `feed` from 0.055 to 0.04 in the source. The spots should grow into stripes.
- Change `kill` from 0.062 to 0.055. The pattern becomes broader and more maze-like.
- Lower `STEPS_PER_FRAME` to 1. The pattern develops much more slowly, and you can watch individual cells change.
- Change the starting density of seeded patches. More seeds means the pattern meets itself sooner and produces finer detail.

## Recap

Three ideas together produce everything you see: diffusion (spreading), reaction (A converts to B on contact), and imbalanced rates (A spreads faster than B). That is enough to grow the biological-looking patterns in this sketch.
