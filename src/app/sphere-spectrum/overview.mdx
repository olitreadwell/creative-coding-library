# Sphere Spectrum

An icosphere whose surface erupts into colored peaks driven by your microphone, with each latitude band responding to a different pitch range.

Learners see how an audio FFT (fast Fourier transform) signal drives 3D geometry in real time, using log-frequency banding to match how pitch works perceptually.

## What you see

- A smooth, slowly rotating sphere at rest.
- After enabling the microphone, the sphere surface pushes outward into peaks where sound is loud.
- Cool blue means quiet; hot red means loud.
- Bands near the top of the sphere respond to bass; bands near the bottom respond to treble.

## What it teaches

- **Vertex displacement:** each point on the sphere surface is pushed outward along its normal direction by an amount proportional to the sound energy in that point's frequency band.
- **Log-frequency banding:** pitch is logarithmic, so raw FFT bins are grouped into bands with geometric spacing. This maps bass and treble more evenly.
- **Envelope (attack/release):** each band's energy follows a fast-attack, slow-release curve. This makes the sphere punchy without flickering.
- **Refs over state:** audio data changes 60 times per second. Using React refs (not state) avoids re-rendering the component tree on every frame.

## Try this

- Enable the microphone and hum at different pitches. Watch which latitude of the sphere spikes.
- Snap fingers near the microphone. The spike should be brief and sharp, then fade.
- Raise the audio slider for more dramatic spikes. Lower it for a gentle shimmer.
