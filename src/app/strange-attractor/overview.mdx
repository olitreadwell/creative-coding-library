# Strange Attractor

A shape that appears when one rule is applied millions of times to its own output, each point plotted very faintly so the shape builds up from density.

Learners see the de Jong attractor, a concrete example of chaos: tiny changes in four numbers produce completely different but still structured figures.

## What you see

- A glowing figure built from millions of faint dots.
- Bright regions show where the path visits most often.
- The figure changes shape when you move the sliders or press the arrow keys.
- Clicking "Randomize" jumps to a completely different figure.

## What it teaches

- **Iteration:** the rule takes a point and produces the next point. Applied millions of times, the visited points trace the attractor shape.
- **Density rendering:** each point is plotted with very low opacity. Where the path visits often, faint marks stack up into bright regions.
- **Chaos:** small changes in the four parameters (a, b, c, d) lead to completely different figures, even though the rule structure stays the same.
- **Keyboard accessibility:** the canvas has `tabIndex` and `role="application"` so arrow keys adjust parameters without a mouse.

## Try this

- Click the canvas to focus it, then press the left and right arrow keys. Watch the figure morph in real time.
- Hold an arrow key to sweep a parameter continuously. The figure breathes and shifts.
- Click "Randomize" and compare the new figure to the previous one.
