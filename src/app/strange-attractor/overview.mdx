## What this is

A strange attractor is the shape that appears when you follow one simple rule
over and over. Start at a point, apply the rule to get the next point, plot it,
and repeat millions of times. The dots never settle on one spot and never fully
repeat, yet together they trace a sharp, detailed figure.

Click the canvas to focus it, then press the arrow keys to morph the shape in
real time. The sliders change the same four numbers that define the rule.

## The big idea in plain words

This sketch uses the de Jong map. Given a point (x, y) it computes the next
point with two lines of sine and cosine:

```
nextX = sin(a * y) - cos(b * x)
nextY = sin(c * x) - cos(d * y)
```

Four numbers (a, b, c, d) fully decide the figure. Nudge one a little and the
whole shape reorganizes. This is the heart of chaos: a tiny change in the rule,
or in where you start, leads somewhere completely different, yet the result is
not random noise. It has structure.

We draw each point very faintly. Where the path visits often, faint dots stack
up into bright regions, so the image is really a map of how much time the system
spends in each place.

## Why it matters

Attractors are a friendly door into chaos theory, the maths behind weather,
fluid flow, and population swings. For creative coding they are a gift: a few
lines of arithmetic produce endless, detailed, organic art. They also show a
useful pattern, density rendering, where overlapping faint marks build tone
without any shading code.
