# Tutorial: Superformula

By the end of this tutorial you will understand what each parameter controls and why integer values of `m` produce symmetric shapes.

## Predict

The formula uses `m * angle / 4` inside the cosine and sine terms. When `m = 4`, the formula repeats itself after a quarter turn (90 degrees), producing four-fold symmetry.

If `m = 5`, how many times does the formula repeat in a full 360-degree turn? What shape do you expect: a star with 4 points or 5 points?

## Run

1. Open the sketch with the default Flower preset (m=5). Count the petals.
2. Switch to Star. Compare the sharpness of the points to the Flower.
3. Switch to Gear (m=19). Count the teeth if you can.
4. Switch to Blob. Notice the soft, rounded shape produced by `n1` below 1.

## Look closer

- The curve is sampled at 720 points, stepping the angle from 0 to `2 * pi`. Connecting those points with `lineTo` draws the full closed shape.
- The scale is computed from the canvas size so the shape always fills the available area, regardless of window dimensions.
- The auto-morph adds `sin(t * 0.4) * 0.6` to `n1` each frame. The `t` value comes from `useAnimationFrame` and is in seconds, so the breathing cycle is about 15 seconds long.
- When `sum` in the formula equals zero, the function returns 0. This prevents a division-by-zero error at certain angles.

## Break it

- Pick Flower and drag `m` to 3. The shape should become a 3-petal flower.
- Pick Star and drag `n1` to 0.5 (below 1). The shape should invert into a different star-like form.
- Set `n2` and `n3` both to 0.5. The shape becomes a smooth, inflated circle with soft lobes.
- Try `m = 6, n1 = 100, n2 = 100, n3 = 100`. The shape approaches a regular hexagon.

## Recap

The superformula maps one angle to one radius. Sample it at 720 angles, convert each to x and y with cosine and sine, connect the dots. Four numbers control everything from a circle to a complex gear.
