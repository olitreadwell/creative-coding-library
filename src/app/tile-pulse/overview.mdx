## What this is

Tile Pulse fills the screen with a grid of geometric shapes. Each tile animates on its own looping cycle, and a travelling wave ripples across the grid so the motion looks alive even before you touch it.

Move your cursor near a tile and it reacts: nearby tiles scale up and rotate toward you. Click anywhere to send a ring of animation spreading outward from that point. Hold the mouse down and drag to leave a continuous trail of disturbance.

## The big idea in plain words

Every tile has two things: a **base animation** and an **influence value**.

The base animation is a looping wave. Each tile is offset in time by its position in the grid, so the wave travels diagonally across the canvas like a ripple in fabric.

The influence value measures how much the pointer or a ripple is affecting that tile right now. It comes from a simple rule: tiles close to the cursor or to the ripple front get a high value; tiles far away get zero. The exact shape of that dropoff (strong at the centre, fading smoothly to nothing at the edge) is called a **radial falloff**.

When you click, a ripple ring expands outward at a fixed speed. As the ring crosses each tile, the tile briefly gets a high influence value, then returns to its base state. The result looks like a stone dropped in water, but made of geometry.

## Why it matters

This sketch brings together three patterns that come up constantly in interactive creative work:

- **Phase offsets.** Giving each tile a slightly different start time in the same loop is how you get a travelling wave from identical objects with identical rules.
- **Influence fields.** A function that maps distance to a 0-1 strength value is one of the most reusable ideas in generative code. The same pattern controls particle attraction, lighting falloff, and sound volume.
- **Event-driven ripples.** Expanding rings are a classic way to make a click feel like it has weight and spread. The ring is not drawn on screen; its position and age are used only to decide how much each tile should react.

The Tutorial tab shows the code behind each of these three ideas.
