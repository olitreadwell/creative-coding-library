# Webcam ASCII

Your live camera feed redrawn as a grid of text characters, where each character is chosen by how bright that area of the image is.

Learners see the three-step camera pipeline (request, sample, draw) and how measuring ink coverage ranks characters by visual density.

## What you see

- A grid of characters replacing your camera image in real time.
- Dark areas of your face or background fill with dense characters like `@` or `#`.
- Bright areas fill with light characters like `.` or nothing.
- Nothing runs until you click "Enable camera."

## What it teaches

- **getUserMedia:** the browser API that requests camera access. It always prompts the user for permission.
- **Glyph density:** each character is measured by drawing it on a tiny canvas and counting the filled pixels. That measurement ranks characters from lightest to heaviest.
- **Luminance:** a weighted combination of red, green, and blue that matches how bright the human eye perceives a color. Green is weighted most heavily.
- **Mirroring:** the camera is flipped horizontally so the image reads as a mirror, not as a camera facing you.

## Try this

- Switch "Glyphs" to "Blocks" for a chunkier, more geometric look.
- Set "Cell size" to minimum for near-photographic character density.
- Raise "Contrast" in a well-lit room to push mid-tones toward darker characters and make the image pop.
