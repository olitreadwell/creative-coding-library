## What this is

Your webcam, redrawn live as a coarse grid. Each cell looks at one patch of the
picture and replaces it with a mark whose weight matches that patch's brightness.
Pick the mark with the style control:

- **ASCII**: dense characters (`@`, `#`) for dark patches, light ones (`.`, `'`)
  for bright patches.
- **Blocks**: the same idea with block characters, for a chunkier look.
- **Circles**: a dot per cell, sized by brightness, for a halftone feel.

Nothing leaves your browser. The camera only starts after you press the button.

## The big idea in plain words

A camera frame is just a grid of pixels. If you shrink that grid down to one
pixel per cell, each pixel becomes the average brightness of a patch. Map that
brightness to something you can draw, a character or a circle, and you have
turned a photo into a mosaic.

The trick for text is ranking the characters by how much ink they use. A space is
empty, a period is light, an `@` is heavy. Sort them, then a dark patch picks a
heavy character and a bright patch picks a light one. For circles it is simpler:
brighter patch, bigger dot.

This is one sketch made from two earlier ones, an ASCII camera and a dot mirror,
because they share almost everything: grab the camera, sample it small, draw one
mark per cell. Only the final mark differs, so it is a single style switch.

## Why it matters

Image-to-symbol mapping is the heart of a lot of generative art: halftone,
stippling, pixel-sorting, ASCII film. Once you can sample an image into a small
grid and decide what to draw per cell, you can swap the "what to draw" for
anything: letters, shapes, your own glyphs, even other images.

The How it works tab walks through the code.
