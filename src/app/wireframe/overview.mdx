## What this is

A solid shape, like a cube or a many-sided ball, spinning in space. It is drawn
on a flat canvas, but it reads as 3D: the near side looks bigger and brighter,
the far side looks smaller and dimmer. Drag it with your mouse or finger to spin
it yourself.

Nothing here is real 3D. There is no 3D engine. It is plain Canvas 2D plus a
little maths that fakes depth convincingly.

## The big idea in plain words

Every corner of the shape is a point with three numbers: x (left/right), y
(up/down), and z (near/far). Two steps turn those numbers into a picture.

**Rotate.** To spin the shape we nudge every point around an axis. The formula
uses sine and cosine, the same pair that draws a circle. Apply it each frame and
the whole shape turns together.

**Project.** A screen is flat, so we have to flatten the points. The trick that
sells the illusion is perspective: divide a point's position by how far away it
is. Near points (large z) barely shrink, far points shrink a lot. That single
divide is why railway tracks appear to meet in the distance, and it is what
makes this drawing look 3D.

To finish the look we draw the far edges first and the near edges last, and we
tint each edge by its depth, so the shape never looks like a flat tangle of
lines.

## Why it matters

Rotate, then project: that is the heart of every 3D renderer, from a phone game
to a film. Doing it by hand in a few lines shows there is no magic inside the
fancy libraries, just sine, cosine, and one division.
